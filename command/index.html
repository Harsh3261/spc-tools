A surveillance audit is shorter than recertification. The auditor samples part of the system rather than every clause, but the list below is checked at every one.

Checked at every surveillance audit

From the rules certification bodies work to (ISO/IEC 17021-1). Have these ready whatever else the audit plan says:

  1. This year's recertification findings: fixed, and the fix shown to work. Usually checked first.
  2. Internal audits done as planned, with findings followed up.
  3. Management review held, with all required inputs and recorded decisions.
  4. Customer complaints and how they were handled.
  5. Quality objectives: results, and what you're doing where they're missed.
  6. Continual improvement, with evidence.
  7. Changes since the last audit, including your appointment as manager.
  8. Use of the certificate and logo on your website, quotes and letterhead.

Plus whichever processes the plan lists for this visit, e.g. machining, welding, testing, purchasing.

Where cylinder shops get written up

These are the usual weak spots in a shop that machines, welds, assembles and tests cylinders. Walk each one before the audit. The Clauses tab has detail on each.

7.1.5Test stand and torque tools. Pressure gauges or transducers on the test bench, and torque wrenches for gland and piston nuts, out of calibration or not on the calibration list.
7.5Drawing revision at the station. Old drawing or BOM at welding, honing or assembly after an ECN.
8.5.1Welding control. No weld procedure at the station, or welder qualifications expired or missing.
8.5.1Outsourced special processes. Chrome plating, nitriding or heat treatment with no validation evidence from the supplier.
8.5.2Traceability. Serial number not linked to the test record, or tube/rod heat numbers lost at the saw.
8.6Test records. Pressure, hold time or result missing; the tester didn't sign; the gauge ID isn't recorded.
8.5.4Preservation. Rods without protection, ports open, rust on stored components.
8.7Rework. Rod sent out for re-chrome or a tube re-honed without an NCR or disposition.
8.4Material certs. Mill certs for chrome bar and tube filed but never compared to the spec.
7.2Competence. Test bench operators or CMM users with no training record.

How an auditor thinks

A certification body (CB, or registrar) checks two things: that your QMS meets ISO 9001, and that people actually follow it. In Canada, CBs are usually accredited by SCC or another IAF member. The auditor isn't inspecting cylinders or trying to fail you. They report what the evidence shows.

Behind every question is one of three tests:

1
Do you have it? A process or procedure covers the requirement.
2
Do you do it? Records show it happening every time.
3
Does it work? Results show it's effective, and problems don't come back.

They sample and follow trails, e.g. from a customer PO for a welded cylinder through to the test record and the calibration certificate of the test stand gauge. The Practice tab walks through four of these trails.

Your role as management rep

ISO 9001 doesn't require a job titled "management representative". Top management assigns QMS responsibilities (5.3) and stays accountable (5.1.1), and the auditor will interview them directly. In practice, you're the main contact. On the day you:

  • Agree the plan and timings, and make sure the right people are there.
  • Guide the auditor, or assign a guide who stays with them the whole time.
  • Keep a request log: what was asked for, who got it, what was shown.
  • Note possible findings as they come up, and bring evidence before the closing meeting.
  • Accept findings professionally, then own the response.

ISO 9001:2026

Published 16 September 2026. 2015 certificates stay valid until the transition ends on 30 September 2029. It updates the 2015 edition rather than replacing it, and the changes are moderate:

  • Quality culture and ethical behaviour in leadership (5.1.1) and awareness (7.3).
  • Risks and opportunities handled separately (6.1).
  • Planning of changes expanded (6.3).
  • Climate change considered in context and interested parties (4.1/4.2). This came from the 2024 amendment, so you may already have it covered.
  • Continual improvement (10.1) strengthened, core terms defined in the standard, and a much larger explanatory Annex A.

Ask your CB when they plan to do your transition audit and which edition your next audit will use. Clauses that change are tagged 2026.

Words you'll hear

Objective evidence
Something the auditor can see: a record, a document, an observation. Opinion doesn't count.
Audit criteria
What you're audited against: ISO 9001, your procedures, customer requirements, and applicable law.
Audit trail
Following one job through linked records to see whether the system holds together.
Nonconformity
A requirement not met, graded major or minor.
OFI / observation
Opportunity for improvement. Not a nonconformity, and no formal response required.
Correction vs corrective action
Correction fixes this instance (recalibrate the gauge). Corrective action removes the cause so it doesn't happen again.
Documented information
"Maintain" means a controlled document (procedure, instruction). "Retain" means a record (proof something happened).
Concession
Authorised acceptance of product that doesn't meet requirements. Usually what your deviations are (8.7).
Special process
A process whose result can't be fully checked afterwards, e.g. welding, plating, nitriding, heat treatment. It must be validated (8.5.1).
CB / AB
Certification body (audits you) and accreditation body (audits the CB, e.g. SCC, ANAB).

Timeline

Prepare

From about two months out to the closing meeting, and after it.

6–8 weeks before

  • Get the audit plan from your CB: dates, auditor, scope, edition (2015 or 2026), and which processes they'll visit.
  • Re-read the recertification report. For each finding, check the correction, root cause, corrective action and evidence it worked are all on file.
  • Internal audits. This year's audits done as scheduled, findings raised and moving. If an area is behind, audit it now.
  • Management review. Held since recertification, or due? Minutes should cover every required input (Clauses → 9.3).
  • CAPA / NCR log. Nothing overdue without a documented reason and a new date.
  • Tell the CB about significant changes: new management rep, reorganisation, new test stand or welding cell, scope changes.

2–4 weeks before

  • Calibration sweep. Every gauge is on the list and in date: bore and thread gauges, micrometers, profilometer, CMM, test stand gauges and transducers, torque wrenches. Anything found out of tolerance has a product impact assessment.
  • Documents at the point of use. Walk the cells: current drawing and BOM at the saw, lathes, welding, honing, assembly and test. No uncontrolled copies.
  • Welding. Weld procedures at the station, welder qualifications current, and the welding certification on file if your customers require one.
  • Competence. Training matrix covers new hires, CNC operators, welders, test bench operators and CMM users.
  • Suppliers. Approved list and re-evaluations, including chrome bar, tube, seal kits and outsourced plating, nitriding and heat treatment. Mill certs checked against the spec.
  • Objectives, complaints, risks. Current data, actions where you're off target, and the risk review done this year.
  • Certificate and logo. Website, quotes, email signatures.

Final week

  • Brief the people likely to be interviewed: supervisors, welders, test bench operators, inspectors, purchasing, shipping. Use the Practice drill. Don't script them.
  • Shop walk. Quarantine area labelled, every part's status clear, rods protected, ports plugged, aisles clear.
  • Audit folder: org chart, process map, scope, quality policy, objectives with data, MR minutes, internal audit schedule and reports, CAPA log, calibration list, supplier list, recertification report and closure evidence.
  • Logistics: a room, PPE for the auditor (safety glasses, boots, hearing protection near the test stand), screen access to your ERP, and a named runner to fetch records.
  • Top management has the opening and closing meetings and their interview slot in their calendar.

Audit day

OPENING
15–30 min. Introductions; the auditor confirms scope, plan, timings, confidentiality and how findings are reported. Ask anything unclear here, e.g. "Will you share possible findings at the end of each day?"
AUDIT
Interviews, shop-floor tour and record checks, process by process. The guide stays with the auditor, the runner fetches records, and you log every request.
FIX
Small problem? Fix it on the spot, e.g. swap an old drawing. That's a correction. It may still be written up, because the question is why it happened.
WRAP-UP
Daily summary of concerns. This is when to bring evidence you couldn't find earlier.
CLOSING
Findings read out with clause and evidence. Ask for clarification and present evidence you have, but don't argue. Confirm the response deadline and the recommendation.

After

  • Log every finding in your CAPA system the same day, with an owner.
  • Send your response by the CB's deadline (Findings tab).
  • Schedule an effectiveness check for each finding. The next auditor will ask.
  • Take the results to the next management review, where they're a required input.
  • Write down what you'd do differently next time while it's fresh.

Watch out for

Answering: the rules

  1. Listen to the whole question. Ask for a repeat if needed.
  2. Answer what was asked, honestly and briefly. Don't volunteer side stories, and never hide anything.
  3. Show, don't tell. "Let me show you the record."
  4. "I don't know" is fine, followed by "…but [name] owns that" or "…let me find out."
  5. Never guess. One wrong answer makes the auditor dig deeper.
  6. Avoid "always" and "never" unless records prove it.
  7. Pausing is fine. So is correcting yourself: "Sorry, it's a 12-month interval."
  8. Don't argue. Ask which requirement it's against, and show evidence calmly.

Problems you found yourself count in your favour

If the auditor finds something already on an NCR, CAPA or internal audit finding, show it. A problem caught and being fixed shows the system working, and often won't be written up.

Useful phrases

"Just to check: you're asking how a new drawing revision reaches the welding cell?" "Let me pull up the record rather than go from memory." "That's owned by our purchasing lead. I'll bring them in." "We picked that up in our own internal audit in June. Here's the corrective action." "Which requirement is that against, so we address it properly?" "Is that a nonconformity, or an observation?"

If you're the guide

  • Do stay with the auditor, sort out PPE, keep them clear of the test stand while it's under pressure, introduce people, keep the log.
  • Do let people answer for themselves. If they're stuck: "Who would you ask?"
  • Don't coach or signal answers, or steer the auditor away from an area. It's obvious and makes them look harder.
  • Do note every possible finding the moment it comes up.

Follow the trail

Auditors test the system by following one item through linked records. The link at each step is an identifier (PO number, drawing revision, heat number, serial number, gauge ID) that has to match the next record. Where it doesn't, there's usually a finding.

After the closing meeting

Findings and your response

How findings are graded, what happens next, and how to write a response the CB will accept.

Grades

MAJOR
Affects the capability of the QMS to achieve its intended results. Example: no internal audits for a year; cylinders shipping without a recorded pressure test. Several minors against one requirement can add up to a major.
MINOR
Doesn't affect that capability. An isolated lapse: one overdue torque wrench, one unsigned test record, one old drawing.
OFI
Opportunity for improvement, not a nonconformity. No formal response, but deal with it, or it may be a finding next time.

Many CBs raise a minor from the last audit as a major if it wasn't really fixed. Check your CB's rules.

What happens next

  • Minor: you send a correction and corrective action plan; the CB accepts it; the next auditor checks it was done and worked.
  • Major: the CB must verify your correction and corrective action, by document review or a follow-up visit (often at extra cost). If it isn't resolved in time, the certificate can be suspended.
  • Deadlines come from your CB, not from ISO 9001. Commonly about 30 days to respond, with majors closed within about 90. Confirm at the closing meeting.

The response, in seven parts

1
Finding: the auditor's exact wording and clause.
2
Correction and containment: what you fixed now, and what you did about product that may be affected.
3
Extent: did it happen elsewhere? Other gauges, cells, records, suppliers.
4
Root cause: why the system allowed it (5 Whys or fishbone).
5
Corrective action: a system change, with an owner and a date.
6
Evidence: revised procedure, screenshot, training record, photo.
7
Effectiveness check: how and when you'll prove it worked.

Worked example: test stand gauge

Finding
MINOR · 7.1.5.2
"The pressure gauge on test stand 2 (ID PG-04), used to record proof-pressure results for released cylinders, was 6 weeks past its calibration due date."
Correction
PG-04 taken out of service the same day, replaced with a calibrated spare, and sent for calibration. As-found readings were within tolerance across the test range, so test results in that period stand. Certificate attached.
If it had read out of tolerance: list every serial number tested since its last good calibration, assess the effect on each test result, and decide on retest, recall or customer notification.
Extent
All gauges, transducers and torque wrenches checked against the calibration list. Found one torque wrench in assembly not on the list at all; added and calibrated.
Root cause
Test stand instruments were added when the stand was built but were never entered in the calibration system, which only covered inspection-room gauges. Nothing forced new measuring equipment onto the list.
Action
New-equipment checklist now requires every measuring device to get a calibration ID before first use (owner: QA, due 2026-12-15). Due-date labels on all test stand instruments. Test record form now asks for the gauge ID and its due date.
Effectiveness
Internal audit of 7.1.5 covering the test stands and assembly in Q2; target zero instruments in use that aren't on the list or are overdue.

Weak vs strong root cause

✗ "Assembler forgot to torque the piston nut to spec. Assembler retrained."

Retraining only fixes it if training was really missing. The auditor will ask why the process let one missed step through.

✓ "The assembly instruction had no torque verification step and the wrench has no click or record, so a missed torque couldn't be detected before test."

This points at something you can change, such as a sign-off with the recorded value or a mark on the nut, so the fix holds on every shift.

Root-cause checker

Paste a root cause or corrective action before you send it. It flags wording auditors usually reject. Runs on this page only; nothing is saved or sent.

Don't promise what you won't do

Every date in your response gets checked at the next audit. Choose actions you'll actually finish, with real owners. Missing your own CAPA dates can become a new finding.
